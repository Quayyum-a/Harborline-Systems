Guide for agencies

White label software development, explained for agencies.

White label software development means one company does the engineering work and another delivers it to its own client under its own name. This guide covers how the model works, what to agree before you start, and when it fits.

What white label development means

In a white label arrangement, an engineering partner does the technical work behind the scenes. The agency keeps the client relationship, presents the work as part of its own delivery, and stays responsible for the result the client sees. The partner does not market itself to the agency's client and does not contact them unless the agency asks.

It is different from reselling a finished product with a new logo. White label engineering is custom work: features, integrations, backends, mobile apps or automation built to fit a specific project.

Why agencies use it

  • Extra capacity when the in house team is full.
  • Skills the agency does not employ full time, such as backend engineering, mobile development or AI features.
  • A way to say yes to client work without hiring ahead of demand.
  • A monthly arrangement that can grow or shrink with the pipeline.

What to agree before work starts

Most problems in white label work come from unclear expectations rather than poor engineering. Settle these points in writing first.

  • Scope: what is being built and what is not.
  • Access: which repository, tools and communication channels the partner will use.
  • Confidentiality: whether an NDA is needed and how the partner may refer to the work.
  • Ownership: who owns the code and documentation on delivery.
  • Communication: who the partner talks to, how often, and what counts as an update.
  • Hours and pricing: how many hours are included and what happens if more are needed.

When it is not the right fit

White label work suits well defined tasks inside an existing workflow. It is a weaker fit when an agency needs product strategy, a design created from scratch, or a partner to manage the end client directly. In those cases a different kind of agreement works better.

How a small first project helps

A short paid pilot is a practical way to test a new partner on a real task before committing to a monthly arrangement. It shows how the partner communicates, how clean the code is and whether work arrives when promised.

Common questions

Is white label development the same as outsourcing?+

White label development is a form of outsourcing where the partner stays out of sight of the end client. General outsourcing may involve the client dealing with the vendor directly.

Who owns the code in a white label project?+

That should be written into your agreement. Many agencies expect to own the delivered code and documentation, but the terms depend on the contract you agree with your partner.

Can a white label partner work inside our repository?+

Yes, and many agencies prefer it. Working in your repository and workflow keeps code history, reviews and deployment under your control.
