Guide for businesses

How to choose a software development partner.

Choosing who builds your software is one of the most important decisions a business makes. This checklist focuses on questions you can answer before you sign anything.

Start with the problem, not the technology

Write down what is slowing the business down, who is affected and what a good result would look like. A partner can only quote accurately when the problem is clear. It also makes proposals easier to compare, because every partner is answering the same question.

Look for evidence that fits your problem

Ask for examples of similar work and for the specific part the team played. A clear explanation of a past problem and how it was solved is more useful than a long list of logos or vague claims.

  • Have you built something close to this before, and what did you learn?
  • Which technologies would you use here, and why?
  • Who will actually do the work?

Compare scopes, not just prices

Two quotes with different prices often describe different work. Check what each one includes: testing, documentation, deployment, handover and a period of fixes after delivery. A written scope with an agreed outcome, timeline and price protects both sides.

Check how they communicate

You will spend more time talking to your partner than reading their code. Find out who your contact is, how quickly they reply, how often you will see progress and which working hours overlap with yours. Time zone differences are manageable when expectations are set early.

Plan the handover and what happens after

Software needs care after launch. Ask who owns the source code, whether documentation is included and what support exists for bugs and small changes. A partner that plans the handover at the start is easier to keep, or to leave, when the project ends.

Warning signs

  • Pressure to sign before the scope is written down.
  • No clear answer on who owns the code.
  • Firm promises about results that depend on things outside their control.
  • Unwillingness to start with a small, paid first task.

Common questions

Should I choose the cheapest quote?+

Not automatically. Compare what each quote includes, and weigh the price against the clarity of the scope, the partner's communication and the plan for handover.

Is it better to hire a developer or work with a partner?+

It depends on how steady the work is. A full time hire suits constant, long term needs. A partner suits project work, specialist skills or changing demand.

What should a software proposal include?+

At minimum: the problem being solved, what will be delivered, what is excluded, the timeline, the price and what you receive at handover.
